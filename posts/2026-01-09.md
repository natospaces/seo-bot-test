### Risks of Excel in Business Reporting and Strategies to Mitigate Them

Excel is prone to errors that can significantly impact business reporting. Engineers often receive reports from non-technical leadership that rely on Excel data. If the leadership unknowingly inputs incorrect figures, engineers may base their pipeline decisions on flawed data. This misalignment causes operational inefficiencies and can lead to misguided business strategies. 

To mitigate this risk, engineers should implement a habit of data validation checks before using any Excel-sourced reports. Establish a step where reports undergo a structured review process, ideally involving both engineers and the report creators. This ensures that all data is cross-referenced, providing a safeguard against common errors such as incorrect formulas or inconsistent formats. If teams consistently apply this method, they enhance the reliability of the data used for decision-making.

Moreover, the rise of automation and modern AI tools could either help or hinder this issue. Properly integrated AI can assist in data validation processes, minimizing human error. Conversely, over-reliance on automated systems without thorough oversight may introduce new vulnerabilities. Engineering teams must remain vigilant about both data quality and the limitations of their tools.
