### Managing Excel Reliability Risks in Business Reporting for Optimal Decision-Making

Engineers often use Excel for data analysis, but its reliability can be compromised when non-technical leadership relies on these reports for critical decisions. These reports may contain errors from manual data entry or formula misuse by teams unaware of Excel's intricacies. Such inaccuracies lead to incorrect conclusions, affecting operational strategies and financial forecasting. 

To mitigate these risks, teams must adopt a habit of peer review. Engineers can collaborate with non-technical leaders by conducting regular review sessions for Excel files. During these sessions, they should validate data sources, check formulas, and ensure interpretation aligns with operational goals. This habit fosters transparency and builds trust, which leads to more accurate reporting.

Additionally, while modern AI tools can assist in data validation, they also pose risks if misunderstood. Tools can generate insights that may not account for nuanced business contexts, leading to misinformed decisions. Therefore, it is essential that engineers maintain traditional checks alongside innovative solutions. Only through consistent review and collaboration can organizations ensure that Excel remains a reliable tool for decision-making.
