### Common Excel Mistakes That Undermine Business Reporting Reliability

Engineers often rely on Excel to present data to non-technical leadership, but a frequent mistake arises from improper formula usage. When engineers create complex links between cells, errors can propagate unnoticed. For example, an engineer may link cells from various worksheets without documenting dependencies. As a result, leaders making decisions based on this data may unintentionally overlook inaccuracies, leading to misguided strategies.

Non-technical leaders rely on the clarity of reports to guide their decisions. If these reports contain undetected errors or inconsistent data, the business risks allocating resources inefficiently or misjudging market conditions. Trust in the data is eroded, leading to decision-making paralysis or, worse, misinformed actions that can harm the organization’s reputation and bottom line.

Furthermore, while modern AI and automation tools can help streamline data processing and reduce human error in Excel, they can also exacerbate issues if not implemented correctly. If AI systems auto-generate reports based on flawed Excel data, the risk remains unchanged. Consequently, fostering a culture where engineers and business leaders collaborate effectively on data integrity is crucial for reliable reporting, making downstream decisions safer.
